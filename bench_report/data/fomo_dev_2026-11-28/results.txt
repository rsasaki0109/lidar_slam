Reading
  - In this forest, summer or winter, G22's odometry is not where it loses: it is at or below upstream on all four.
  - The backend's map refinement helps on all four (-1 % to -25 % at keyframe stamps).
  - The loop closure hurts on all three drives with a loop, even where the loop edge is better than the odometry.
    Anchored at the start, the optimised trajectory changes only in its last ~30 keyframes (addendum 2).
  - G22's frontend memory (0.7-1.0 GB) and speed on 128 beams remain the known cost of voxel 0.25 m (chapter 65).

  The per-point time of a RoboSense frame is monotonic in azimuth (-3597 deg/s, 0.1001 s a turn): the time field is
  right. rko_lio reads it as relative float seconds (process_timestamps.cpp).
  Leishen instead of RoboSense (same drive, G22, rig from transforms.json; 4 rings, forward field of view, per-point
  time spans only 7.8 ms of a 0.1 s frame):  ATE 0.4796 scale 1.00019; deskew false 0.4566 scale 1.00025.
  The Leishen's odometry has the UTM scale, but its ATE is too large for the scale to be precise.
  Direct check, robot standing at each drive's start (p10_fomo_range_check.py, 5 frames of each LiDAR, Leishen
  points against RoboSense planes): residuals are dominated by a 2.5-6.5 cm offset that changes with the range band
  (the LiDAR-to-LiDAR extrinsic, most likely); the fitted range ratio is 1.0001-1.0014 and not stable. Inconclusive.

Reading (after 5 and 6)
  - G22's odometry is about 0.1 % long against this reference in both seasons; upstream about 0.06 %. Removing it
    would take ~20 % off G22's ATE. Whether the RoboSense, the extrinsic or G22 causes it is not settled; voxel 1.0
    takes part of it away (0.99923).
  - The loop closure's harm is not a weighting or convergence problem of the backend.
