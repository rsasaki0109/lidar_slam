FoMo (Foret Montmorency) as forest development data, summer and winter (written 2026-11-28, before any LiDAR, IMU,
odometry or ground-truth byte of a FoMo trajectory is read)

Why
  The maintainer asked for better accuracy in forests, in both summer and winter, with no in-house rig. The forest
  results so far are four GrandTour missions in November (report chapters 57 and 62): G22 is the best method on all
  four (HOEB-1, KAEB-1, LMB-1, ALB-1 without reference spikes), at 1-2 cm. They are one legged robot, one season, and
  opened. Nothing in this repository measures a forest in summer foliage or under snow, or a wheeled robot in a forest.

Family
  FoMo (Boxan et al., IJRR 2026, doi:10.1177/02783649261491481; CC BY 4.0, s3://fomo-dataset, no account needed).
  Boreal forest 80 km north of Quebec City; a Clearpath Warthog (skid steer); six routes named by colour (red, blue,
  green, magenta, yellow, orange) repeated on 12 deployments from 2024-11-21 to 2025-11-03, from -19 C with more than
  1 m of snow to summer foliage. LiDARs: RoboSense Ruby Plus (128 beams, spinning) and a Leishen hybrid solid-state;
  IMUs: VectorNav VN-100 and an Xsens. Reference: PPK GNSS from three Emlid M2 rovers and a static base, TUM file
  gt.txt, with per-pose covariances (gt_covariance.csv). Per-frame files on S3 (robosense/<us>.bin = float32 N x 6:
  x, y, z, intensity, ring, time), IMU CSV (t [us], wx wy wz [rad/s], ax ay az [m/s^2]), calib/transforms.json.
  No FoMo file was ever in this repository's records.

What was read before this plan
  The bucket listing, LICENSE.txt and sizes.json (bytes per sensor per trajectory); for data/2025-08-20/red_*: the
  key names, CHANGELOG.md, calib/ (transforms.json, imu.json, the two Allan-variance reports, key names of the rest),
  and the header row plus the first two rows of vectornav.csv, xsens.csv and odom.csv (odom: zeros at the start).
  The README and the loader functions of norlab-ulaval/fomo-dataset-tutorials (file formats). Not read: its section
  Q5 (localization methods across seasons, which contains results), the paper's result tables, any gt.txt, any LiDAR
  frame.

Split, from route names, dates and sizes only
  Development (opened by this plan), routes red and blue, two winter and two summer deployments:
    winter  2025-01-29  red (RoboSense 14.2 GB)   blue (28.7 GB)
    winter  2025-03-10  red ( 9.7 GB)             blue (26.4 GB)
    summer  2025-06-26  red ( 8.3 GB)             blue (15.0 GB)
    summer  2025-08-20  red ( 8.7 GB)             blue (15.8 GB)
  Phase A is the four red drives; phase B adds the four blue drives (streamed from S3 into the bag, never stored raw,
  because a blue drive does not fit /dev/shm on this VM).
  Holdout routes, not read in any deployment (no LiDAR, IMU, odometry, calibration or reference byte): green and
  magenta. They keep a forest route whose geometry no setting has seen, in every season, for a later blind run whose
  selection is written before it is fetched.
  Within-route holdout, not read for now: red and blue on the other eight deployments.
  Out of scope by size on this VM: yellow and orange (55-124 GB of RoboSense per drive).

Per-rig inputs (fixed here, before any run)
  LiDAR: RoboSense Ruby Plus, topic /points, frame robosense. Base frame base_link; base_link -> robosense is a
    180 deg yaw with zero translation (transforms.json). Poses are written in base_link.
  IMU: VectorNav VN-100 (vectornav.csv), m/s^2, extrinsic from transforms.json via zedx_left. Chosen over the Xsens
    by the rig's own Allan reports (gyro angle random walk 0.17-0.21 vs 1.76-1.89 deg/sqrt(h); bias instability
    1.8-2.4 vs 13-16 deg/h). Tactical-gyro keys (G21): off (bias instability above 1 deg/h). Gravity window: off
    (no static multi-orientation calibration).
  The convention of "to"/"from" in transforms.json is checked once on phase A's first drive by the frame check of
  earlier families (gravity direction in the IMU at rest, LiDAR ground plane); the check uses no reference.
  Conversion: one rosbag2 MCAP per drive with sensor_msgs/PointCloud2 (x, y, z, intensity float32; ring uint16;
  time float32 seconds from the frame stamp) and sensor_msgs/Imu, written with rosbags. Every point is kept.
  The odometry CSV and the Leishen are not used in phase A.

Methods
  G22 (configs/sota_v7/rko_lio_global_G22_2026-11-10.yaml) with the rig keys above, through the pip package
  (python -m lidarslam odometry, byte-identical to the ROS offline node on HILTI exp14, ros-free-core M3), then the
  default backend (python -m lidarslam backend). Both outputs are scored: frontend odometry and backend trajectory.
  Upstream RKO-LIO (PyPI 0.4.x, its defaults) as the reference point, since chapters 65-66 found that it beats G22 on
  a 128-beam rig outdoors. Other rivals only if phase A shows a gap that needs them.

Score
  ATE in metres: positions of the estimate interpolated to gt.txt stamps, SE(3) Umeyama alignment without scale,
  RMSE, over the reference poses inside the estimate's time span. Also, as in the blind runs, the value without
  reference spikes, and the reference's own covariance summary per drive (median and 95th percentile horizontal
  sigma), because PPK under canopy can be worse than a LiDAR odometry. Per drive: RTF and peak RSS. Winter and summer
  are reported as separate geometric means; neither season's mean may get worse for the other to get better.

What this can decide
  Phase A is a diagnosis: where G22 loses in a forest, by season. A change to G that comes out of it needs its own
  preregistered rule with the usual development screening (MapsHD 8, exp04, Odyssey 7, as for G23 in chapter 65),
  and an accuracy claim in forests needs the holdout routes run blind. FoMo can never be a fresh family for the
  general claim tiers after this plan, only its unread routes can be a within-family holdout.

Record
  Each later step is appended here before it is run; results go to results.txt next to this file.

Addendum 2 (2026-11-28, after phase A's first runs on the four red drives; before the backend replays below)
  Seen (results.txt): G22's frontend is at or below upstream RKO-LIO on all four red drives (ATE 0.069-0.080 m vs
  0.071-0.095 m). The score script interpolated sparse keyframe trajectories linearly in time at the reference stamps,
  which is wrong where the speed changes (winter 2025-01-10: 0.288 m for keyframes equal to the odometry); it now has
  --at, and backend trajectories are scored at their keyframe stamps with the odometry scored at the same stamps.
  Then the loop closure makes all three drives that have a loop worse (one loop each, start to end), although the loop
  edge is closer to the reference than the odometry on two of them (2025-03-10: relative position error 0.174 m in
  the odometry, 0.036 m in the edge). Anchored at the start, the optimised trajectory moves only in its last ~30
  keyframes; the middle of the route is unchanged. Adjacent-edge information is uniform (hop k: 1e4/k translation,
  1e6/k rotation, hops 1-5); the loop edge has 200 / 1e5.
  Diagnosis to run (no decision follows): record each red drive's backend input once (map --backend-input), then
  replay the backend (python -m lidarslam backend) with one group changed at a time: the loop information (constant
  translation 200 -> 2e3, 2e4), the adjacent rotation/translation ratio, and the optimiser iterations, to find why the
  correction is not spread along the loop. A backend change to the default needs its own preregistered rule on all
  development data of the walking preset; FoMo alone decides nothing.

Addendum 3 (2026-11-28, after the backend replays of addendum 2; before the frontend variants below)
  Backend: reproduced the backend's optimisation from pose_graph.g2o with a stand-alone g2o tool (identical ATE).
  It is converged (10 and 100 iterations give the same result), and no reweighting helps: loop translation
  information x10 / x100, adjacent rotation information x0.1 to x0.001, adjacent translation x0.1 / x10 all stay
  worse than no loop (red 03-10 / 08-20 / 09-24: 0.080-0.082 / 0.085-0.086 / 0.069-0.070 vs 0.0698 / 0.0786 / 0.0667).
  The loop edge is not the problem; the odometry's error is not of the kind a loop closure removes.
  Found: a Sim(3) fit gives G22's odometry a scale of 0.99891-0.99912 on all four red drives (winter and summer);
  upstream 0.99916-0.99955. Sim(3) ATE is ~20 % below SE(3) ATE for G22 (e.g. 0.0802 -> 0.0617). The reference is
  UTM zone 19 about 244 km west of the central meridian at about 645 m: its grid distances are about 1.00023 x the
  ground distance (point scale 1.00033, height reduction 0.99990). So against the ground, G22's odometry is about
  0.12 % too long and upstream about 0.08 %, consistently.
  Diagnosis to run (red 2025-09-24, then the other three if a variant moves the scale): G22 with one change each,
  scored as before plus the Sim(3) scale:
    deskew false; voxel_size 1.0 (upstream's; G22 has 0.25); per-point time reversed within a frame (a check of the
    converter's time field); the LiDAR frame stamp moved to the frame's end; the Xsens instead of the VectorNav.
  The scale is "explained" by a variant that brings it within 0.0003 of 1.00023 on all four drives. A change to G
  or to the converter that comes out of this needs its own rule; FoMo alone decides nothing.

Addendum 4 (2026-11-28, the maintainer chose to pin down the scale first; before the runs below)
  Question: where does G22's ~0.1 % odometry scale on the RoboSense come from (results.txt section 6)?
  Step 1, red 2025-09-24, G22 with one key changed toward upstream's default (scale and ATE each):
    vox05 voxel_size 0.5 | vox1 voxel_size 1.0 (done: 0.99923) | minr1 min_range 1.0 | noinit initialization_phase
    false | kp15 icp_keypoint_voxel_multiplier 1.5 | noclose close_scene_max_median_range 0 | it100 max_iterations 100
    + convergence_criterion 1e-5 | jerk3 max_expected_jerk 3 | mcd1 max_correspondence_distance 1.0
  Step 2, sensitivity, same drive: the RoboSense points scaled by 0.9988 in the converter (diagnostic only, never a
    per-rig input chosen from the reference). Expectation if the scale passes 1:1 into the odometry: G22's scale near
    0.99896 / 0.9988 = 1.00016.
  Step 3: every step-1 key that moves the scale by more than 0.0002 toward 1.00023, run on the other three red drives.
  Reading, fixed now: a key "explains" the G22-specific part if it brings G22 to upstream's scale (within 0.0001 on
  all four drives). The common part (G22 and upstream both below 1.00023 by 0.0007-0.0011) is attributed to the
  sensor side only if no algorithm key moves it; that is a hypothesis for the rig, not a finding, until a check
  without the reference (a calibration target, the dataset authors) confirms it.

Addendum 5 (2026-11-28, after steps 1-2 of addendum 4; before step 3)
  Correction: gt.txt is not UTM. Its easting (255853 m) fits NAD83 / MTM zone 7 (EPSG:32187, central meridian
  -70.5 deg, false easting 304800, k0 0.9999): pyproj places the first pose at 47.3221 N, 71.1475 W (Foret
  Montmorency); UTM 19N would put it at 72.23 W. Grid scale there 0.999929, height reduction at ~645 m 0.999899:
  a correct odometry shows a Sim(3) scale of about 0.99983, not 1.00023 (addendum 3 and results.txt section 6 used the
  wrong projection). Against 0.99983: G22 is 0.07-0.09 % long, upstream 0.03-0.07 % long, the Leishen run 0.04 %
  short. The reading thresholds of addendum 4 are taken against 0.99983.
  Step 1 (red 2025-09-24): only the sampling resolution moves the scale: voxel_size 1.0 +0.00027, keypoint
  multiplier 1.5 +0.00016, voxel_size 0.5 +0.00011; initialization off -0.00006; five other keys 0.
  Step 2: the RoboSense points x 0.9988 give scale 1.00016 (expected 0.99896 / 0.9988 = 1.00016): a range scale
  passes 1:1 into the odometry; ATE 0.0688 -> 0.0502.
  Reference-free LiDAR-to-LiDAR Sim(3) ICP at standstill (p10_fomo_lidar_sim3.py): not identifiable with the
